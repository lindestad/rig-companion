# Eye pointer alignment

The **Eye tracking → Align eye pointer in VR** button closes the SteamVR dashboard if it is open, waits five seconds, then starts a temporary overlay. With the headset on, look at the small white center dot inside the active purple ring. Faint dots show all positions on the two flatter concentric rings. The active ring brightens and grows slightly during acquisition; the center dot stays the same size and position. This feedback means capture is underway, not that alignment is already accurate. The first target stays up for an extra two seconds before sampling begins, giving you time to find it. Follow the center target, then the inner and outer rings. Seven additional check targets follow, ending at the center again. Allow about a minute, longer if a target needs a retry. Markers along the bottom show progress within each phase. Keep the headset in its usual position on your face. Press Escape on the keyboard to cancel.

The overlay texture is 4096 × 2800 pixels; its circles use smoothed edges. The target and layout keep the same size in the headset as the earlier texture, apart from the smaller active ring.

The helper gathers distinct, fresh eye samples in a recent window for each target. It reacquires after a tracking gap or a large gaze excursion and checks both angular spread and movement between the beginning and end of the window. A difficult target gets one automatic retry. The raw-gaze proximity check during calibration is deliberately generous so that a real offset is not mistaken for looking away. Validation has no target-proximity gate: an inaccurate but steady fixation must be measured rather than silently excluded.

The existing v3 correction still smoothly averages nearby measured offsets. Before saving it, the helper collects six fresh points between the original targets and repeats the center. On exactly the same fresh samples, it compares the proposed correction, the saved profile (if present), and raw gaze. The app displays their average angular error, the worst new target's error, and the raw center shift since the first target. These are validation estimates assuming you looked at the targets, not independently measured hardware accuracy. The helper's standard output also lists each target's mean sample error, median-position bias, 90th-percentile spread, and the number of polls that returned new gaze.

A failed, rejected or cancelled run keeps the prior profile. A successful run atomically saves `%APPDATA%\Rig Companion\eye-pointer-calibration.json` and asks the driver to reload it. A failed reload restores the previous file. The driver reloads the profile when it starts again. The v3 file format, interpolation bandwidth and driver are unchanged.

The initial capture limits are at least 35 samples spanning 1.2 seconds, no gap over 150 ms, a maximum 1.5-second window, 90th-percentile spread within 1.5°, and early/late median separation within 0.6°. A movement over 3° from the recent median restarts acquisition. Time spent redrawing feedback does not count toward a fixation. Validation rejects a center shift over 1°, mean error over 2.5°, or any target's mean error over 4°. Against both raw gaze and the saved profile, it also rejects an average regression exceeding the larger of 0.35° or 15%, or a target regression exceeding the larger of 0.8° or 30%. These are practical starting tolerances, not Dream Air specifications; fresh headset results should guide any future tuning.

This corrects only the dashboard pointer enabled with F19. The SteamVR eye tracking component and the gaze data used by games or foveated rendering are unchanged. It is an alignment aid, not a replacement for Pimax/Tobii tracker calibration. Remove the profile file and restart SteamVR to return to unadjusted eye aim.

The helper only runs during alignment. It creates no virtual controller and no persistent overlay. `eye-calibrate.exe --preview` displays a center target for six seconds without recording gaze or changing the profile.

The independent validation and generous acquisition gate follow [Tobii's calibration guidance](https://www.developer.tobiipro.com/commonconcepts/calibration.html). The repeated center checks for a changed mapping during the session; [SR Research distinguishes drift checks from applying a drift correction](https://www.sr-research.com/support/thread-213-post-212.html). This helper reports or rejects drift rather than automatically shifting the saved map. No temporal cursor smoothing has been added: validation is needed to distinguish inaccuracy from jitter first.
